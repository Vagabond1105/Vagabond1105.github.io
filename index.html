Why it exists

Tracking lifts is essential for long-term progress, whether the goal is strength, size or both. Most trackers are either too basic to be useful or so bloated they get in the way of the training.

Plates is my answer as a lifter: a tracker that stays out of the way during a session, goes deep when you sit down to analyse years of history, and doesn't charge for things that cost nothing to run. Your training lives in a file you own, not a row in someone else's database.

Product decisions

Free means the whole app

Every module except the AI runs on your own machine at no cost, on desktop and phone. No account, card or email needed.

Your data is a file you own

Local-first. Training data stays on the device it was logged on unless you choose the cloud.

Pay for what has running costs

Sync, the web app, cloud storage and AI cost real money to serve, so those are the paid parts. Nothing else is.

No ads, ever

The business is the subscription. There is nothing to sell to advertisers.

AI proposes, you approve

Budgets that can't run away

What it's for

A Z-pinch is a column of plasma carrying a large current. The current's own magnetic field squeezes it, which is how lightning channels and some fusion experiments confine plasma. It is also unstable: it can bend into a helix, pinch into beads, sag, or tear apart.

The goal was a desktop laboratory where you watch that column live in 3D, turn the knobs a real experimenter would turn, and trust that what you see is what the laws of physics predict rather than an animation that merely looks plausible.

v3 adds the part I cared most about: after every run, the program tells you which failure happened, why, and what to change. Students get plain language. Researchers get the fitted rates, stability criteria and energy tables underneath.

Using it

  1. Pick a textbook runOr set current, axial field, resistivity, cooling and gravity yourself.
  2. Read the forecastThe predictor names the likely outcome before you press Start.
  3. Watch and steerGPU volume rendering with tracer particles. Current, resistivity, cooling and gravity change live.
  4. Read the reportThe run is labelled, timelined and explained against the textbook criteria.

Design decisions

Classify first, then predict

Label finished runs from their history, then train a second model to forecast from the starting settings alone.

No generated text

Explanations come from a fixed library of 21 hand-checked write-ups, filled in with the run's own numbers.

Two audiences, one report

Plain language for students, full data underneath for researchers, including CSV export.

One night of compute

The whole dataset had to come from a single overnight sweep on a laptop.

Show doubt, don't hide it

Low confidence is displayed. A label within 15% of a threshold is flagged as borderline.

Disagreement is a bug report

When the classifier and the physics rules disagree, that gets investigated. Two rule weaknesses were fixed this way.

How it works

Single-fluid resistive, viscous MHD in 3D, solved with a finite-volume scheme: each cell is a bank account of mass, momentum, energy and magnetic field that only changes by what flows through its faces. Every run starts from an exact Bennett equilibrium.

Validated against problems with exact answers: Sod shock tube, a circularly polarised Alfvén wave (order → 1.85), resistive and viscous decay, a fully driven pinch with mass and energy budgets below 10⁻¹², and Bennett equilibrium.

How it was built

v1 · Jan 2026Architected and designed by me, implemented by a collaborator

I specified the physics, module structure, setup/live parameter split, tracer particles, 3D view and the rule that energy must add up. The architecture held, but the numerical core couldn't work: energy grew ×2,700 in 3 steps and the state hit NaN within 36. It stays in the repo with a full post-mortem.

v2Rewritten by Claude Code

A validated finite-volume solver, numba-compiled physics and GPU ray-cast volume rendering. One step went from 19.9 s to 9.6 ms, memory from 12.7 GB to under 1.2 GB.

v3 · to Oct 2026Agentically engineered by me

I set the design and made the decisions above before any code was written, then directed Claude Code phase by phase through the sweep, the models and the docs, making the calls along the way.

Limitations

  • Dimensionless code units, not a model of a specific machine.
  • Single-fluid MHD: no separate electron and ion temperatures, no Hall effect, no kinetic effects.
  • The sweep used one 32 × 32 × 48 grid. 59 of 60 runs repeated on a finer grid kept their label, but growth rates shift with resolution.
  • The write-ups were checked against the data but haven't been reviewed by a plasma physicist.

Funky Radical Electrodynamics Engine: a 2D classical electrodynamics sandbox, built without any physics libraries. Place charges, give them mass and velocity, press Start, and Coulomb's law takes it from there. Accelerating charges radiate, and you can watch the light leave them.

Why I built it

A self-directed project alongside my degree, to learn how a physics simulation earns trust. The plan had five phases, from fields on a 2D grid to light. Each phase in the notebook has its aims, a planned architecture, and a frank account of how it actually went.

Using it

  • Right-click to place a charge and set its charge, mass, velocity and restitution. Drag to move, double-click to duplicate.
  • Start, Pause and Reset. Reset returns everything to its starting state.
  • Toggle radiation, trails, wall restitution, time step and frame rate. A live readout shows KE, PE, radiated energy and drift.
  • The potential is drawn as a heatmap, and as a 3D surface in its own window.

The integrator journey

  1. Explicit EulerEnergy grew steadily and orbits spiralled outward.
  2. RK2Smoother, still drifted.
  3. RK4Accurate short-term, but not symplectic, so long runs drifted.
  4. Velocity-VerletSymplectic. Energy stays in a bounded band.

The central numerical result: a lower-order method wins because it respects the structure of the problem.

Radiation, by design

Perpendicular to the force

Wavefronts leave as 90° arcs, the half-power width of the sin²θ pattern.

Paid for by kinetic energy

KE + PE + radiated = E₀ stays a live check.

Coloured by frequency

Red to violet, ranked by each charge's orbital frequency.

The real speed of light

One pair of arcs per cycle of the motion.

What it gets wrong

  • Electrostatic forces only, acting instantly. Fine while v ≪ c.
  • A 400 px softening length makes the force much softer than Coulomb at screen scale.
  • Non-relativistic Larmor radiation, with an energy drain in place of a self-force.
  • Classical physics: a real atom would collapse. The README lists every simplification.
